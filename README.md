## Redline Rush

A roguelike traffic-weaving racer built in **Godot 4** (GDScript). You drive an endless four-lane highway: close passes and late lane changes at speed score the most, and each stage ends in a boss race.

### Open and run

1. Install **Godot 4.3 or newer** (the standard build, not .NET) from https://godotengine.org/download.
2. In the Project Manager choose **Import**, pick this folder's `project.godot`, then press **Run** (F5).

There are no asset files to download: the cars, road, trees, UI and sounds are all generated in code.

Every player and boss car has its own body design: a hatchback, a sport sedan, a notchback and a fastback muscle car, a compact performance saloon, a mid-engined supercar, a rear-engined sports coupe and a V12 hypercar. The paint has a clear coat that reflects the sky and horizon, metallic flake that catches the sun up close, and tinted glass that turns into a mirror at shallow angles. Wheels have big alloys with concave spokes, low-profile tyres, brake discs and calipers. Lamps have projector lenses and LED running lights. Cars further than 40 m away switch to a lighter model, so the highway stays smooth on phones.

Each car has its own engine sound, generated live from a small engine model: cylinder count (from the hatch's four to the Last Exit's twelve), RPM through six gears with real shift points, throttle load, pops when you lift off and turbo whine and blow-off on the turbo cars. On and off throttle use different combustion sounds, every firing varies slightly, the exhaust pipe resonates, and you hear crank rumble at low revs, gearbox whine and an ignition-cut bark on upshifts. The HUD shows your gear and an RPM bar under the speedometer.

### Controls

| Action | Phone | Desktop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accelerate | Hold the accelerator pedal (bottom right) | Up / W |
| Brake | Hold the brake pedal (bottom left) | Down / S / Space |
| Steer | Swipe left or right anywhere else on the screen (how far your finger moves from where it landed is how far you steer) | Hold Left / Right |
| Pause | Pause button, or Android Back | Esc / P |

The car drives freely, like a real one: let go of the steering and the wheels self-centre, but the car keeps going the way it points, so you straighten up yourself. Nothing pulls it back into a lane. **Steering assist** (Settings › Controls, off by default) gently straightens the car up to the road when you let go, without snapping it to a lane.

### Cameras

Tap **CAM** (top right) to switch between **Chase**, **Hood** and **Cockpit** views. The cockpit is a full interior:
- **Instruments:** a real instrument panel: a rev counter and a speedometer with numbered, backlit faces (km/h with a small mph ring, and fuel and temperature bars; the rev counter's scale and red band follow each car's engine), a bezel on each, tapered needles that stand off the face and throw a shadow on it, a faint reflection on the glass, a window between the dials with the gear and a large speed readout, a row of warning lamps and, on the track cars, shift lights; and a lit sat-nav and radio screen with its own night-mode map. Each car has its own dial design in its own colours: modern, sport, classic cream with serif numerals, bold retro white, plain, or segmented track style.
- **Controls:** a steering wheel that turns: an oval-section leather rim, fuller where the hands go, with a seam of contrast stitching round it, tapered spokes with lit buttons (or slotted metal spokes on the classic wheels), and a rounded airbag hub with a badge, held by gloved hands whose fingers wrap round the rim; and a gear lever that moves through the gears.
- **Cabin:** round turbine, slim strip or bladed air vents (by car), a demister slot along the windscreen, knurled climate knobs with a row of buttons, trim strips in carbon fibre, open-pore wood or brushed metal, seats with headrests, a rear bench, door panels, sun visors and a mirror. Plastics and leather take a soft sheen from the sky, and the roof and pillars darken where they meet the glass, like a seal in shadow.

### Settings

Open **Settings** from the main menu or the pause menu. It has four tabs, each split into labelled groups with a short explanation under every option: **Controls** (steering method, sensitivity, steering response, steering assist, tilt direction, dead zones, pedal size, vibration, on-screen layout), **Camera** (view, adjustments, camera shake), **Display & Audio** (orientation, graphics, speed units, mirror, sound) and **Developer**. The game keeps drawing behind it, so camera changes preview live.

The main menu is your **garage**: a dark showroom workshop with your car standing on a glass-topped turntable, its reflection in the floor, walls of backlit slats, long light bars in the ceiling and an amber LED ring round the turntable. The paint and glass reflect that room (while the garage shows, the car shaders switch to a studio environment through the global shader parameter `env_studio`), and the camera frames the car in whatever part of the screen the menu panel leaves free (above the panel when the phone is upright, to its left when it is on its side). It eases between views when you change tab (a front three-quarter shot for **Cars**, higher for **Paint**, close on the front wheel for **Wheels**, from behind for **Upgrades**), circles slowly round a car nobody is touching, swoops in when the garage opens, and a different car slides in from the side with a flash of light. Drag to look around it. The main menu's panel shows the car's name, fuel and stats (with your upgrades) and leads with **Start run**, with **Cars**, **Workshop** and **Settings** under it and **How to play** on its own screen. **Cars** shows one car at a time, with arrows to go through all twelve (the locked boss cars too, to look at), the car's numbers with how each compares with the car you drive, and one button for what you can do with it: select, buy (with what you are short of, if you are) or locked (with the boss to beat). The **Workshop** works on your current car: **Upgrades** (fitted to that car only: each car is upgraded separately, and older saves keep the upgrades they had on every car they own), **Paint** (13 colours plus factory, free) and **Wheels** (six designs and six finishes, or factory). Paint and wheels are saved per car and used in your runs. The garage has its own camera, lights and render layer, so the highway never shows through, and the road simulation pauses while you're in it. It is lighter to draw than the workshop it replaces: the room is a few dozen merged meshes and the floor reflection uses the light version of the car, so a frame is about 330 draw calls and 160,000 triangles (`--perf`, on software Vulkan) against 780 and 400,000 before, and the sun's shadow maps, which drew the car twice for nothing, and the 3D view behind the world map are switched off. The pause menu leads with **Resume** and keeps **End run** apart at the bottom, and the run-over screen leads with **Run again**.
- **Camera:** choose a view and set its field of view, height, distance (forward/back for hood and cockpit) and angle. **Reset this view** restores the defaults.
- **Imported cars:** the Kestrel, Vanta GT, Bruiser, Copperhead, Halcyon, Blue Static, Night Marshal and Last Exit, and the traffic SUV, van and lorry, are now your own models (`tools/models/` sources, `assets/models/car_*.mesh`, each with a lighter distance version). Each keeps its cabin style, and the cockpit is fitted from the model's measured roof, windows and wheels and then clamped inside its real shell, so nothing pokes through. The traffic lorry is a three-axle tractor and trailer (its box takes the traffic colour, the cab a second colour, plus the fleet name), and traffic models get a lighter high-detail mesh and a decimated distance version so a busy road stays fast. The generated hatch and saloon remain for traffic until their models arrive.
- **Car models:** every player, boss and traffic car (apart from the imported Comet and Hornet, and the van and lorry) is forged as a detailed mesh by `tools/carforge.py`: smooth curved bodywork with rounded bumpers, real glass with black window surrounds and frit, LED head and tail lamps with projectors and light bars, mesh, slatted or kidney grilles, lower intakes, bonnet louvres, side intakes, diffusers, shut lines, flush handles, mirrors, wipers, shark-fin aerial, exhaust tips, and each car's spoiler or wing. Boot lips and ducktails are swept into the bodywork itself, every detail follows the rounded nose and tail, lamps wrap only the corners, and grilles and intakes are sized to the nose they sit on. Nearby cars use the full-detail version and switch to a light one beyond 22 m; your own car is always full detail.
- **Cockpit view, like looking out of a real car:** the roof, A-pillars and door frames are the car's own bodywork seen from inside (so they sit exactly where the model has them, with clean edges round the windscreen and side windows, a headliner colour per car, and dimmed at night), with the rear-view mirror turned towards you. The seat goes where the roof is highest and your head just clears it; the camera looks a touch below the road so the wheel and dials are in view. The dash is laid out from the driver's eye: its top ends at the windscreen base, the instrument pod and dials sit under the sight line over it, and the wheel sits under the dials, so the road and bonnet are never hidden. The driver's hands rest on the wheel with slim sleeved arms rising from below. Low cars (Halcyon, Blue Static, Last Exit) seat you a little towards the middle, under the highest part of the roof.
- **Interiors:** every car has its own cabin, set in `CABINS` in `scripts/car_model.gd`. Colours, seats (comfort seats or one-piece buckets with harness slots), steering wheel (round, flat-bottomed or a yoke, with two to four spokes and an optional twelve-o'clock mark), screen (a wide screen, a tall floating tablet, or a small radio with knobs), gauge colours, dash trim (aluminium, wood, carbon or body-bright) and extras all differ: blue cloth and a tablet in the Pulse, red-stitched black in the Vector, yellow buckets in the Hornet, tan leather with a two-spoke wheel in the Comet, plain grey cloth and a radio in the Kestrel, beige and wood in the Vanta, black vinyl with a thin chrome-spoked wheel and three dash-top gauges in the Bruiser, white buckets with cyan lighting in the Halcyon, saddle leather and gauge pods in the Copperhead, blue-stitched black in the Night Marshal, electric-blue buckets in the Blue Static, and red leather, a yoke and a roll cage in the Last Exit. Every cockpit is measured against its body's real roof, sides and tail and kept inside them. The detail is made two ways: the dial faces, the screens' maps and the trim finishes are artwork baked by `tools/make_cockpit_art.py` into `assets/textures/cockpit/` (the colours come from `CABINS` and the rev counter's scale from each car's engine redline, so they cannot drift from the game), and everything with a shape (needles, bezels, the wheel, the hands, vents, knobs, trim strips) is built in `scripts/cabin_parts.gd`. Measured with `--perf`, the player's car went from 56 objects and 135,700 triangles to 36 objects and 140,600 triangles, so the cabin is no dearer to draw than before. A yoke sits lower than a round wheel, so its top bar does not hide the speed readout.
- **Starter cars:** every new garage starts with the **Pulse Hatch**, a hot hatch with a roof spoiler, and the first car to buy is the **Vector GS** ($600), a turbocharged sports saloon with a boot spoiler. Both are imported 3D models with see-through glass, the full cabin and cockpit view, their own number plates, brake lights and indicators. Their texture's shut lines, handles and fuel flap are carved into the bodywork as real trim, the black band low on the sides is body colour above a gloss-black sill, and the cockpit is measured against the real shell (roof, glass and sides) so nothing pokes through. After them come the **Hornet Compact** ($800) and the **Comet Coupe** ($1,000), also imported models, then the Kestrel ($1,200). Older saves get the Pulse added and keep the cars they had.
- **Driving physics:** cars steer like real ones, and respond quickly: the front wheels turn in fast and the car starts to move over in about a quarter of a second. The wheels turn in within a frame, the nose answers within about 20 ms and the direction of travel follows the nose as fast as the tyres can push it round (the steering may lean on about 1.4 times the car's grip stat at **Standard** response), so the car starts to move over at once and a full-lock steer-and-counter-steer lane change takes about 1.2 s (it was 1.4 s) at any speed; harsh inputs at speed still give a small slide instead of an instant sideways jump. The car's side of this is `scripts/steer_model.gd`. The body rolls and pitches about the middle of the car at hub height (like real suspension), so the wheels stay inside their arches. The car turns only by rolling forward on its turned front wheels (you can see them turn), so it can't rotate or slide sideways when it's stopped, and at a crawl it needs plenty of lock to move over. The front wheels turn at a limited rate, the available lock shrinks with speed, and the tyres grip only up to a limit, so asking for too much at speed makes the car run wide. You steer it yourself the whole time: there is no lane snapping. Braking hard and driving on the shoulder cost grip. The body leans out of turns, and traffic, bosses and police also move over only as fast as they're rolling forward. **At low speed** the car steers like a real one too: below about 70 km/h the front wheels take about a second to go from straight to full lock at a crawl (and 0.9 s to swing from one full lock to the other, 0.4 s at 30 km/h), the tightest turn is about 5.6 m in radius, the sideways acceleration it can ask for at a crawl is limited to about 5.5 m/s² (a bit over half a g, for a standard car), and the nose and the direction of travel answer more slowly the slower you go; from 70 km/h upwards it steers exactly as before (`SteerModel.blend` is the fade between the two, and `tools/steer_low_test.gd` prints the numbers).
- **Steering:** **Stick**: a floating analog stick that appears wherever you put your finger (away from the pedals) and steers as far as you push it left or right (past its edge it follows your finger, so turning back answers at once); **Swipe** anywhere on the screen (the start point trails your finger the same way); an on-screen **Wheel** you turn with your thumb (a short 90-degree travel; let go and it runs back to centre like a real one, faster the faster you go); **Buttons** for left and right; or **Tilt** (tilt the phone; the angle you hold it at when a run starts or resumes counts as straight, 16 degrees is full lock, and **Tilt direction** flips it if it feels backwards). Every method turns the front wheels: full input asks for about the most grip the tyres have at your speed, so it feels the same at 60 and 200 km/h, and small inputs are softened for fine corrections. Tilt is read through a one-euro filter (steady when your hand is still, almost no lag when it moves) with a short look-ahead and the gyroscope's help, and every method answers a quick movement a little ahead of itself, so a decisive flick gets a decisive turn-in while slow, steady input is left exactly as it is. **Steering response** (Relaxed, Standard, Sharp) sets how hard the car leans on its grip and how strong that look-ahead is: try Sharp if it still feels slow. **Dead zones** (Settings › Controls, one slider each for tilt, stick, wheel and swipe) set how far a control has to move before the car starts to steer: bigger ignores small wobbles, smaller answers sooner, and full lock stays at the same place whatever the setting. The defaults are what the controls always used: tilt 0.9°, stick 3.5% of its travel, wheel 3%, swipe none; the control you are steering with is highlighted.
- **Feeling what the car does:** the steering control shows the car's state: a marker (above the stick, round the top of the wheel, or on a slim bar above the speedometer when you tilt) shows which way the car's nose points, so after a lane change you steer back until it is in the middle, and the ring or bar warms from white through amber to red as the tyres reach their limit. The wheel in the cockpit turns with your hand at any speed. Vibration also buzzes like a rumble strip on the verge (quicker the faster you go) and trembles faintly when the tyres are at their limit.
- **Screen:** **Portrait** or **Landscape**. The game locks to the one you choose, so turning the phone never switches it by accident.
- **Graphics:** **High** (full shadows, anti-aliasing, speed blur at the screen edges and a colour grade), **Medium** or **Low** for older phones. On the first run on a phone the game checks the frame rate and steps down a level if it's struggling.
- **Frame rate:** **Screen** (the default) draws a frame every time the display refreshes; **60 FPS** or **30 FPS** limits it. On a 90 or 120 Hz screen the limit makes the phone run a lot cooler. The game runs at the same speed at any rate (the simulation steps by the time between frames), but a lower rate is less smooth and takes coarser steps, so it is off by default. Whatever you choose, a menu, the garage or the pause screen that nobody has touched for a moment and a half is drawn 30 times a second (nothing moves there but a slow turn of the car) and at full rate again as soon as a finger goes down.
- **Developer tools:** **Infinite lives**, an **FPS counter**, **Show hit boxes** (outlines drawn on every car), **Unlock all cars**, **Max all upgrades**, **+$100,000 cash** and **Reset progress** (tap twice; keeps your settings).
- **Rear-view mirror:** on by default, at the top of the screen. Turn it off, or set **Mirror size**, in Settings. Move it in the layout editor.
- **Pedal layout:** **Edit pedal layout** lets you drag the gas, brake, steering wheel or buttons and the mirror anywhere on screen (**MIRROR −** / **MIRROR +** resize it there). **Swap pedals** puts the gas on the left, and **Reset pedals** returns them to the bottom corners.

### How a run works

- **Lives:** 3. Touching a car, the rival or the guardrail costs one. The Spare chassis upgrade adds more.
- **Scoring:** points appear in a short list just under the score counter, clear of the road and of the rear-view mirror: small and white for a few points, and bigger and warmer as the amount climbs (13 px for a handful, 22 px at 1,000, 27 px at 10,000, turning from white through amber to orange). A pass is worth more the faster you go. Passing within 1 m is a **near miss** and within 35 cm is a **razor pass**. A lane change right beside a car is a **close cut**. Each of those adds to a combo multiplier (up to x8) that resets after 3.5 s without one. Above 120 km/h you also earn points every second.
- **Stages:** fill either the stage's distance bar or its points bar to trigger the next encounter.
- **World map:** **Start run** opens a road-atlas map (shaded terrain with coastline and shallows, beaches, rivers, woodland, hills and snowy peaks, each region coloured by its highway's country; winding highways with interstate shields; a little lit skyline for every city, padlocked until you can reach it; compass and scale bar) of 15 cities (Port Halden, Ashford, Millbrook, Cedar Falls, Red Mesa, Dustwater, Silver Lake, Ironvale, Northgate, Granite Pass, Copper Ridge, Sunset Bay, Lakeshore, Neon Heights and Summit City) joined end to end by 14 highways (I-10 to I-80). Each highway is its own endless run through its own country (green farmland, autumn woods, desert flats, northern pines, coast road or frosted plains, with matching ground and sky colours), and each one further along raises score targets (+14% per highway) and makes its bosses harder: the bosses of the first highways are easy and those of the last are as hard as the game gets. You start with only the first highway open: beat its final boss, Last Exit (boss 4), and the highway on to the next city opens. Tap a highway to see its country, your best score on it and how hard its bosses are (EASY on the first highways up to BRUTAL on the last), then **Drive**. Locked highways are grey dashes, and the stage sign shows which highway you're on.
- **Day and night:** time runs while you drive, one real minute to two game hours (a full day in 12 minutes), shown by the speedometer. Runs start in the afternoon; the sun crosses from east to west, sunset turns the sky gold and the hills purple, and night brings a moonlit sky full of stars. As it gets dark every car's headlights and tail lights come on (wide cone-shaped pools on the road that fan out from the lamps, and real beams on yours), the median street lamps switch on and light the carriageways, and road studs and reflector posts shine. Dawn brings it all back. The boss theme, *Cold Iron Street*, fades in from 50 seconds into the track and plays through each boss or police encounter, fading out over about 6 seconds when it ends.
- **Boss warning:** filling the distance or points bar no longer stops the game. A 10-second countdown runs on the stage sign while you keep driving and scoring ("BOSS OR POLICE?" when it could be either), with beeps for the last three seconds, and then the boss (or the police) closes in from behind. The traffic around you is left exactly as it was when the boss arrives: nothing is moved or removed. Fuel stops are moved out of the way of the countdown and the race.
- **Police pursuit:** each trigger has a 30% chance to be a police car instead of the boss. It starts 80 m behind you and chases you down, gaining at most 9 m/s (it was 16, which put it on you in about 4 s on an open road; now it takes about 7½ s in light traffic and 9½ s in the heaviest, and it picks up speed more gently), and tries to ram you: getting hit costs a life, but surviving 40 seconds untouched earns an extra life. Either way, the next trigger in that stage is always the real boss. The police hunt for gaps in traffic to get at you, and highway traffic gets out of their way: a car in the police car's lane (or the one it is moving to) starts to make way when the police will be on it in 7 seconds or are 60 m behind it, moves to the right if there is room (else the left), asks for less room than an ordinary lane change (more only for a faster car coming up behind), and when no lane has room just now it speeds up or slows down to line up with a gap rather than slowing in the way. Cars do not pull into the lane the police are coming up in, and a car that has signalled and finds the lane it is moving into filled up waits at the lane line (and gives up after a second) instead of driving through the car there. In 8 test pursuits through the heaviest traffic the police were held up by cars in their way for 1.9 s on average (5.3 s before, and up to 19 s), with no cars driving through one another in them.
- **Boss race:** you have 2 km to out-score the boss before the finish gantry. Bosses score exactly as you do (near misses, razor passes, close cuts, combos and speed) and drive hard to get them: they dart for gaps in traffic, brake late, pick lanes with slow cars to skim past and hug the car they're passing. Each boss is better at it than the last (Copperhead is a rookie, Last Exit a legend, and every lap of the four bosses gets harder again), and so is each highway's set of bosses: on I-10 all four are rookies or merely skilled and their driving is scored at 62% of what it earns, rising highway by highway to I-80, where the bosses are the hardest there are and score a quarter more than their driving alone earns (`Data.boss_skill` and `Data.boss_edge`). In test races over 2 km (the player a simple autopilot that scores about 3,000, three runs each) the first boss of I-10 scored about 1,700 points, the last boss of I-10 about 4,400, of I-27 about 10,700, of I-40 about 16,200, of I-60 about 25,900 and of I-80 about 34,000. Win and you get prize money, choose 1 of 3 upgrades for the rest of the run, and the boss's car goes on sale in the Garage. Lose and it costs a life, followed by a rematch.
- **Traffic:** sedans, hatchbacks, SUVs and pickups come in many colours, wheel styles and makes, each with its own number plate. Delivery vans are modern high-roof panel vans with flush cargo sides, a sliding door, twin rear doors with windows, a tall slatted grille and big commercial mirrors, and the lorries are cab-over tractors with box trailers carrying a fleet livery and name, mud flaps, side guards and reflective tape.
- **Back gestures:** swipe in from the left or right edge of the screen to go back one step in any menu (settings, garage, map, help, game-over, replay, refuel), the same as the Android back button. While driving it does nothing, so it can't pause by accident.
- **Impact feel:** every knock rocks the body on its springs away from the blow (a damped spring that settles in half a second), and hard hits freeze time for a moment.
- **Hit boxes:** a car's hit box is its real outline seen from above, made from its model by `tools/make_hulls.py` (the lower body, below the mirrors, as a convex outline of 14 corners; the lorry is two pieces, box and cab), so a tapering nose, rounded tail corners and wheel-arch flares count and the empty corners of a rectangle don't. The outline is turned the way the car points, which also decides where it meets the guardrail (a nose turned towards it touches first) and gives the gap of a near miss. Contact is found by the separating-axis test, which also works out where the two cars touched. **Show hit boxes** in Settings > Developer (or `--hitboxes`) draws the outlines.
- **Knocks:** a blow between two cars is worked out the way it really is: an impulse at the point where the outlines touch, along the line of the contact, with a little bounce (a crumpling car keeps about a fifth of its closing speed) and the paint's friction along the surface, shared out by weight and by how far the point is from the middle of each car. A square rear-end shove only shoves (you lose about the speed difference, the other car gains it, nobody spins); a hit at the corner spins the car it hits and swings your nose (the tyres wear that swing off, you steer back); a side swipe pushes both apart and drags the slower car along. Lorries are 3.5 times as heavy, vans 1.5 and SUVs 1.25. A car that is hit is a wreck from then on: it is no longer driven, slides, spins and comes to rest by itself, and if it slides into other cars or the guardrail they take the blow too. A glancing scrape along a barrier costs little speed, hitting it at an angle a lot. Hard knocks throw off bits of bodywork, trim and glass.
- **Wrecks:** a wrecked car is a rigid body on the road, seen from above (`scripts/wreck.gd`). Its four tyres rub on the road: sideways they hold about 1 g however fast it slides, along their own direction they only drag (rolling resistance and what is left of the brakes), and each patch of rubber is pushed by the car's own movement at that corner, so a car sliding sideways scrubs speed off at about 9 m/s², a spin on the spot dies in a fraction of a turn, a car that is only slightly crooked straightens up, and a fast one that is turned right round slides on backwards, all without anything being faked. No car spins faster than about 3 rad/s (a turn in two seconds). On its roof or side it slides on the body instead (about 0.6 g). Wrecks lie where they stop, so a car can end up across a lane.
- **Last-life crash:** losing your final life is the same physics with nothing held back: the blow is worked out from the real contact (or the corner of the car that touched the guardrail), the car has lost control as well (at least a shove away from what it hit and a swing of the nose), bits of bodywork, trim and glass fly off, and the wreck smokes once it stops. It spins out in slow motion, leaving tyre marks and smoke. The sliding car collides with traffic instead of passing through it: both cars are shoved and spun by each blow and the cars it hits become wrecks too, while traffic behind stops or goes round. It doesn't always roll. A slide on its own only sometimes trips it, but sliding sideways into another car or the barrier often flips it over.
- **Crash damage:** every knock leaves its mark on the car that took it, on the player's car, the traffic, the rival and the police alike (`scripts/car_damage.gd`). A blow becomes a dent, a point on the car's own body with the way it was pushed, how wide and how deep (a hard knock at the nose or tail also shortens that whole end, and a second knock nearby deepens the first dent instead of making another; at most eight dents per car). Each dent is pressed into a private copy of the car's meshes, so the body, the lamps and the glass crumple together with a ripple in the metal: the surface normals tilt into the dent so the reflections bend round it and the paint scuffs dark. The work is done a few milliseconds a frame (about 3.5 ms at most, shared between however many cars were hit), so a pile-up never makes a long frame and a dent presses in over a few frames like a crumple. The glass nearest the blow cracks (from a knock of 4 m/s, more with a harder one), lamps at the nose or tail go dull when it is 5 or more, the number plate is lost from 6.5, and a knock of 9 or more at a corner of the nose or tail tears the wheel off about half the time: it bounces and rolls away down the road and the car drops on that corner. A car that has taken a lot, or a hit of 8 or more, smokes from its engine, and other cars that are badly wrecked catch fire. Your own view stays clear: the glass over your cockpit cracks at most half way and your car never burns. The garage can show it: `--dent=front:9,left:6`.
- **Notifications:** the police, a boss, a new stage and the rest are slim bands that slide in from the right edge of the screen, stay a moment and slide out again (a red stripe for alerts, green for good news, amber for information), instead of a big banner across the middle of the road.
- **Scoring sound:** the points chime is a short synthesised bell (FM synthesis at 44.1 kHz with a few soft echoes as a little room), tuned to a pentatonic scale so every combo step is a note that fits the last one, climbing as the combo grows; glass has its own breaking sound. They are made in code (`scripts/engine_audio.gd`), and `--save-sfx=DIR` writes them out as WAV files to look at.
- **Running cool:** the game does the same work with less of it, so the phone stays cooler without anything looking or driving differently. Measured without a window, the script side of a frame is about a third lighter and the simulation is bit-for-bit the same (`--state-log` over eight different runs of 80 seconds each, with police, fuel stops, other cars, other highways and heavy traffic, gives identical lines before and after). Drawn with the renderer the phone uses (on a software Vulkan driver, as nothing else was at hand), the pictures are identical to the pixel, except that the sky and the light now follow the time of day in steps and that a menu nobody is touching is drawn 30 times a second (see below). What changed:
  - **Sky and light:** they were set from the time of day every frame, which cost a fifth of the script time (and every change to the sky is work for the renderer too; only the script side could be measured here). Now each of four parts (the sun, the sky and haze, the night look of the cars, the scenery and stars) is set every fifth of a second, one part at a time. A day passes in 12 minutes, so in that time the colours move by well under one step of the 8-bit screen (compared pixel by pixel with setting them every frame: 0.2 to 0.5 of a colour level off on average).
  - **HUD:** a bar's fill, a label's colour and the stage sign's colour are set only when they change (setting one, even to the same value, made the control work out its size and shape its text again, or redraw everything that uses the style), and text that changes once in a while (the speed unit line, the clock, litres, the stage's title) is built once, not every frame.
  - **Cars:** the traffic's look-ahead only considers cars it could react to (nearer than 45 m, using plain arrays); the collision test skips cars whose outlines cannot touch; the wheels of a car are turned in one step, and the body is not moved again while a car stands still.
  - **Sound:** the engine is not synthesised at all while the master volume is muted (the garage, or sound off in Settings).
  - **Menus:** the garage, the main menu and the pause screen are drawn 30 times a second once nobody has touched them for a moment and a half (nothing moves there but a slow turn of the car), and at full rate again as soon as a finger goes down.
  - **Release build:** the debug export carries the debugger and its checks, which cost the scripts some speed (how much was not measured here, only a desktop build was at hand). See Build for Android for the release export (it is signed with the same key, so it installs over the debug one).
  - **Frame rate** in Settings (Screen, 60 or 30): on a 120 Hz screen drawing every refresh is twice the drawing of 60 a second; a limit of 60 halves that work. It is off by default because it is the one thing here that does change how smooth the game looks.
- **Replay editor:** the last 20 seconds are always recorded. Open it with **Watch replay** after a run or **Replay** in the pause menu. Play, pause, step frame by frame, scrub the timeline, play at 0.25x to 2x, and switch between Chase, Hood, Cockpit, Free cam, Keyframes, Trackside and Drone cameras. **Set in** and **Set out** trim the clip, which then loops.
  - **Hide UI** (top right) gives a clean view with no controls, name tags or score messages. You can still move the camera; tap the screen or the faint **Show UI** button to bring the controls back.
  - **Free cam:** drag to swing the camera round the car, pinch to move in and out, and drag with two fingers to pan. The camera follows the car.
  - **Keyframes:** set up a view, then press **+ KEY** to keyframe it at the playhead. Move the playhead, set up another view and add another key. The **Keyframes** camera glides smoothly between them, and keys show as diamonds on the timeline. **− KEY** deletes the key under the playhead, **< KEY** / **KEY >** jump between keys, and **Clear** removes them all.
- **Fuel:** every run starts with a full tank, shown by the gauge under the speedometer (in the cockpit, on the dashboard screen). Driving flat out burns it fastest. Run dry and the car coasts to a stop and the run ends. About every 10 km there's a fuel stop on the right: blue signs 600 m and 300 m before it, and a price pylon, show its prices. Move right into the exit lane and the car pulls in and parks right beside a pump by itself (the first pump it can still stop at; come in too fast and it can't pull in). **Hold to pump** for as much as you want, or **Fill up**, then **Pay & go**. You pay from this run's earnings first, then your bank.
  - Each station sets its own prices. The cheaper cars (Pulse, Vector, Hornet, Comet, Kestrel, Vanta) take regular, the middle ones (Copperhead, Bruiser, Night Marshal) mid-grade, and the fastest (Halcyon, Blue Static, Last Exit) premium. Mid-grade averages $5 a litre more than regular, and premium $15 more. The Garage shows each car's fuel and tank size.
  - Bosses and police pursuits never start near a fuel stop. Choosing the upgrade after a boss clears the road: every car from 80 m behind to 260 m ahead vanishes and nothing new appears for a second, so you can get a hand back on the wheel. The upgrades on offer (three picked at random from this pool) are **Turbo kit**, **Big brakes**, **Sticky tyres**, **Long gears**, **Spare wheel** (+1 life), **Show-off** (+15% points), **Adrenaline**, **Pit crew**, **Crumple zone** (the next hit costs no life), **Roll cage** (1.5 s longer protection after every hit), **Rolling roadblock** (traffic 10% slower), **Eco tune** (25% less fuel) and **Sponsor deal** ($400 per stage as an instant bonus). Beating a boss can offer **Pit crew** (a free full tank) as one of the three upgrades when your tank isn't full.
- **Between runs:** cash from score, distance and boss prizes buys permanent upgrades and new cars. Progress is saved to `user://save.json`.

### Build for Android

The project includes an export preset (`export_presets.cfg`) for a standard APK. It doesn't need Gradle or Android Studio's build system.

1. **Export templates:** in Godot, choose **Editor > Manage Export Templates > Download and Install**.
2. **Java and the Android SDK:** install a JDK (17 or newer) and the Android SDK. Android Studio is the easiest way to get the SDK; you need **build-tools** and **platform-tools** from its SDK Manager.
3. **Editor Settings > Export > Android:** set **Java SDK Path** and **Android SDK Path**. Godot creates the debug keystore itself the first time; if that field is empty, see the table below.
4. **Project > Export:** select **Android** and click **Export Project** to get `build/redline-rush.apk`. Or plug in a phone with USB debugging on and press the Android button in the editor's top-right corner.

Portrait orientation, immersive full screen and the vibration permission are already set.

**If the export fails**, the message at the bottom of the Export window usually names the cause:

| Message mentions | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| export templates / "No export template found" | Step 1, and make sure the templates match your Godot version exactly. |
| Android build template / Gradle | Turn **Use Gradle Build** off in the Android preset (it is off in the included preset). |
| Android SDK path / apksigner / build-tools | Step 3. The path must contain `build-tools/<version>/apksigner` and `platform-tools/adb`. |
| Java / JDK | Point **Java SDK Path** at the JDK folder (the one containing `bin/java`). |
| keystore | Leave the debug keystore fields empty so Godot creates one, or create one yourself: `keytool -genkeypair -keystore debug.keystore -storepass android -alias androiddebugkey -keypass android -keyalg RSA -validity 10000 -dname "CN=Android Debug"` |
| ETC2 / ASTC texture compression | Already enabled in `project.godot`. If it was turned off, re-enable **Rendering > Textures > VRAM Compression > Import ETC2 ASTC**. |

Command-line export, once the editor settings are in place:

```bash
godot --headless --path . --export-debug "Android" build/redline-rush.apk
```

**For playing, export a release build.** The debug export carries the debugger and its checks, which cost the scripts some speed (and so some heat). The release export needs a release keystore: the debug one signs it the same way, so it installs over the debug build as an update:

```bash
GODOT_ANDROID_KEYSTORE_RELEASE_PATH=path/to/debug.keystore \
GODOT_ANDROID_KEYSTORE_RELEASE_USER=androiddebugkey \
GODOT_ANDROID_KEYSTORE_RELEASE_PASSWORD=android \
godot --headless --path . --export-release "Android" build/redline-rush.apk
```

Run `python3 tools/gen_imported.py` |

Smooth splined bodies with rounded noses and tails, glass with black surrounds, sculpted LED head and tail lamps, mesh or slatted grilles, intakes, louvres, diffusers, shut lines, flush handles, mirrors, wipers, exhausts, spoilers and wings. | `scripts/skid_marks.gd` | Tyre marks laid on the road during a crash |
| `scripts/perf_probe.gd` | `--perf`: times the sections of the frame and counts what is drawn (see Test flags) |

Run `python3 tools/make_inner.py` after converting or changing a car model |
| `tools/convert_coupe.py` | Converts an imported car model (`tools/models/*.glb`: the Pulse hatchback, Vector sport sedan, Comet and Hornet) into a game mesh (`assets/models/*.mesh`), mapping extra parts by material (spoilers and mirrors as paint, trim, aero parts, chrome; the model's own calipers are left to the game's wheels), keeping painted number plates as plates and noticing red paint so a red car is not mistaken for a tail lamp. ### Test flags

Arguments placed after `--` on the command line:

- `--autotest`: the car drives itself and the run loops, with status printed to the console. Combine it with `--headless` to run without a window.
- `--fast`: shrinks stage and boss targets so you reach a boss within seconds.
- `--cop`: the next encounter is always a police pursuit.
- `--crash-at=SECONDS`: trigger the last-life crash at that time.
- `--collide-at=SECONDS:KIND[:LIVES]`: at that time clear the road around the player and set up a real collision that the game finds by itself: `KIND` is `rear`, `rear_off` (0.9 m to one side), `corner`, `side` (alongside, drifting in), `truck`, `van` or `rail` (nose into the guardrail); `LIVES` is what the player has left (default 1, so it is the last-life crash). With `--autotest` the blow and then the wreck are logged (`[autotest] impact ...`, `[autotest] crash t=...`).
- `--dent=KIND:STRENGTH[,...]`: the garage car takes knocks (`front`, `rear`, `left`, `right` or `corner`; strength is the change of speed in m/s, 3 a tap and 10 a hard knock), to see the crash damage without crashing.
- `--demo-pops`: shows sample scoring messages from small to big, to see how the text grows with the points.
- `--save-sfx=DIR`: writes every synthesised sound effect into DIR as a WAV file, then carries on.
- `--hitboxes`: draw every car's hit box as lines.
- `--perf[=FRAMES]`: times the sections of each frame (microseconds, averaged) and every FRAMES frames (default 600) prints them with what the renderer was asked to draw: triangles, objects and draw calls for the main view, its shadow pass and the rear-view mirror, and how many triangles the scene holds by kind (`PerfProbe`). Headless it measures the script side; under a window the draw numbers are the real renderer's.
- `--seed=N`, `--shot-frame=FRAME:PATH`, `--state-log=EVERY`, `--start-hour=H`: with Godot's own `--fixed-fps` a run repeats exactly, so two builds can be compared frame for frame. `--seed` fixes the random world, `--shot-frame` saves a screenshot at that frame (rather than at a time), `--state-log` prints the state of the simulation every EVERY frames (the player, the sums over the traffic, the run) and `--start-hour` starts the clock at that hour and lets it run (unlike `--hour`, which holds it). For example, to check that a change did not touch the simulation, and what it did to the script time:
- `--replay-at=SECONDS[:CAM]`: open the replay editor at that time, optionally with a camera (`chase`, `hood`, `cockpit`, `free`, `keys`, `tv`, `drone`).
- `--stations=KM` / `--first-station=M` / `--fuel=FRACTION`: a fuel stop every KM km (the tank range shrinks to match), the first stop M metres in, and starting with that fraction of a tank.
- `--start-stage=N` / `--godmode`: start runs at stage N, and never lose lives (not saved). With `--autotest` this prints each boss race's scores.
- `--quit-after=SECONDS`: exit after that many seconds.
- `--open=garage` / `--open=garage:up`: open the Garage on start (on the Workshop's Upgrades). `--open=ACTION[:ARG]` runs any menu action (`browse:1` for the next car in the Garage, `paint:#c4161c`, `select:vector`, `map`, `settings:camera`...), one after another if repeated.
- `--boss-sk=X`: every boss races at skill X, whatever the highway and stage. With `--route=R --start-stage=S --autotest` each boss race prints its skill, its score generosity (`edge`), the two scores and what the rival did.
- `--shot=SECONDS:PATH`: save a screenshot.
- `--cam=chase|hood|cockpit`: start in that camera view (not saved). `--cam=inspect` looks across the cabin from the back seat.
- `--route=N`: drive highway N (0 to 13), opening it if needed.
- `--hour=H`: hold the clock at that hour (0 to 24) to check the light at any time of day.
- `--open=settings` (or `--open=settings:controls|camera|display|dev` for a tab) / `--open=pedal_edit`: open the settings screen or the pedal editor on start.
- `--car=ID` / `--autospeed=M/S`: test-drive another car (not saved) and set how fast the test driver goes.
- `--steer=swipe|wheel|buttons|tilt|stick` (with `--fake-tilt=DEGREES` to simulate tilting) / `--orient=portrait|landscape`: try a steering method or orientation without saving it.
- `--steer-test=flick|full|lane|pass` (with `--steer-speed=M/S` and `--steer-assist`): a scripted hand drives the real steering on an empty road and logs what the car does (`[steer] ...` lines); `pass` lets the chosen control's own value through, e.g. `--steer=tilt --fake-tilt=2 --steer-test=pass --open=set:dz_tilt=3` checks a dead zone. `godot --headless --path . --script tools/steer_test.gd` measures the steering model and the tilt filter on their own: how quickly the car moves over after a flick, how long a lane change takes, how late and how steady the tilt command is.
- `--idcolor`: draw each traffic car in a flat unique colour with no fog or scenery; with `--burst` it also logs where each car should be, to measure flicker.
- `--demo-pops`: show a sample score message every second, to check where messages appear in each camera view.
- `--burst=SECONDS:COUNT:DIR`: save COUNT consecutive frames from SECONDS onwards (for checking flicker).
- `--showroom` / `--showroom=front` / `--showroom=close` (or `t_rear` / `t_front` for traffic models; `--inspect=t_SHAPE:…` frames a single traffic model): park every player and boss car in a row with the game frozen, to inspect the models.
- `--inspect=CAR:YAWS:PITCH:DIST[:FOV]`: one car on its own, with the camera orbiting it at the given angles in degrees (several yaws separated by commas step every 3 seconds). Combine with `--win=WxH` (and Godot's `--resolution WxH`) for large screenshots.

```bash
godot --headless --path . -- --autotest --fast --quit-after=90
godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 --quit-after 3600 -- --autotest --fast --orient=portrait --seed=7 --godmode --state-log=200 --perf=3600
```
(Run the second one on both builds and diff the `[state]` lines: they must be identical. For pictures: `xvfb-run godot --path . --rendering-method mobile --rendering-driver vulkan --fixed-fps 30 --quit-after 903 -- --autotest --fast --godmode --seed=5 --start-hour=18.7 --shot-frame=300:a.png`, on Mesa's software Vulkan, draws with the same renderer the phone uses.)

- Cockpit camera: the driver's eye is placed under the highest part of each car's roof with room for the head (never inside the headliner or pillars), and the roof, pillars and doors are drawn from the car's real bodywork (`tools/make_inner.py`).
